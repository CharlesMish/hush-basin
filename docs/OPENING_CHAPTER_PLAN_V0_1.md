# Hush Basin — Opening Chapter Narrative Plan v0.1

25 September 2026 · Story planning only · No implementation · Names, occupations and services remain proposals

**The short answer.** Hush Basin's first chapter is a 20–40 minute loop through the whole Basin. The courier meets three people at their own places, carries things that matter to them, and ends the chapter slightly known. By the end:

- Ren's desk can hear the city again.
- Tess has her own counter, and the courier's craft carries her stitching.
- The Quarry crew who dried their socks on the kettle sign a ticket with an initial and tell you to come out sometime.

No plot is announced. The player notices that requests travel farther than they did, and that people have started mentioning each other, and you.

**Basis.** This plan draws on:

- **Charlie's owner play of Narrative Presence Lab v0.1.** Strongly positive: portraits mattered, the situation felt real, story pulled the final drive, and the ending came too soon.
- **One comprehension failure from that play.** Charlie read Ren as Relay, so meeting her at Market felt like a remote call.
- Astra's Arc 2 Shadow Study, the v0.2 bible and the World Blueprint.

One owner test is not validation, and Charlie knows the scripts. The next test needs at least one unfamiliar player.

**Working identity, lightly revised.** *Hush Basin is a courier game about a small working city. Driving is the game; people are the reason. Deliveries change relationships, places and what work becomes possible next.* "Character-driven" promises more story weight than a game that is mostly driving should claim.

## 1. Four rules this plan adds

1. **A face means here.** A portrait character appears only at their own place: Ren's booth, Ivo's bench, Tess's counter. Anywhere else they exist only as text (a ticket, a note, an initial). This fixes the Market confusion, and it keeps "portrait = someone in front of you" true for the whole game.
2. **Every authored leg carries something that belongs to someone.** Repositioning drives can happen; they are never the story.
3. **Seeds use slots that already exist:** receipts, ticket text and one-sentence return lines. Nothing seeded is mandatory.
4. **Voice by grammatical person.** Each voice has its own:
   - Ren talks about herself (*I told, I said, I'll*).
   - Ivo talks about other people (*she, her*).
   - Tess talks to you (*you, your*).
   - The Quarry voice is *we*.

   With several writers, human and model, contributing, sentence structure is a sturdier voice guide than word lists.

## 2. Revised Ren/Ivo opening

**Verdict: yes, this is cleaner.**

- Meeting Ren at Relay matches the mental model Charlie formed on his own.
- It removes the expected drive back to Market.
- It answers his second question structurally: Ren can't call Ivo because her receiver is the broken thing.

The cost is one extra leg. But it is a real delivery, and it gives the game's first drive a long Drive north toward a named person.

Market's lead stays institutional. It doesn't need a named character; it needs a parcel.

**Ren at Relay, first meeting**

1. “I'm Ren. That's a week of requests this desk couldn't hear.”
2. “I told Quarry we'd be answering again today. Ivo's got the receiver—he doesn't know I said *today*.”
3. “These are the last parts. Could you run them to Works? And maybe mention the *today* part.”

This runs about 45 words, the same as before. Ivo's “Ren promised today?” still answers panel 3, and every later beat is untouched. Nobody needs to explain why the parts are at Relay: the customer's order came to the customer.

**Arc 1 driving identity: commitment.** The arc uses the long spine, the outer sweeps, a controlled shelf arrival and one technical approach at Works.

- **Relay ↔ Works.** This is a round trip by nature. Where the current build supports it, advise L4 one way and the northern bypass or A2/East Gate the other, so it doesn't read as the same road twice. Advisory only.
- **Market → Relay.** This makes for a long first delivery. It is open road, which is forgiving, but watch whether new players enjoy it or drift.

Direct play is probably 20–30 minutes. Exploration and optional work stretch it toward 40. Measure it; don't pad it. Between parts the player is free: ordinary paid work and challenges stay on the board, and nothing waits on them.

## 4. Arc 2 — Tess: “Her own counter”

**Human reason.** After a year on a borrowed table in the Depot yard, Tess opens her own mending counter in the existing TEA & MENDING frontage (B11). It's her first day. The courier helps it go right:

- her debt to Depot is squared;
- her biggest regular is supplied;
- her first order arrives at the new door.

Then she notices you.

| | Tess — mending |
|---|---|
| **Impression** | Late 20s; quick, compact. Brick-red work coat, tape measure around her neck, sleeves pinned back. Her one portrait looks down and out of frame, at your craft rather than your face. |
| **Personality** | Sociable, certain, diagnostic. Reads people through what they've worn out. Never hedges. |
| **Wants** | To be a fixture of her street. To owe nobody. |
| **Irritated by** | Makeshift repairs: tape, wire, rope. Ivo is *amused* by clever makeshift fixes; Tess is offended by them. Same stimulus, opposite reactions: that's how a small cast stays distinct. |
| **Do not become** | A cute seamstress, a mother hen, a vendor, a flirt, or a scold about your driving. |

| Leg | Origin → destination; cargo | Why; what it leaves |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Rent** | South counter → Depot: the freight crew's jackets, mended at the new counter | She paid for a year of table space in her own trade. Depot receipt: “Jackets received. Yard's quieter without her.” Depot then offers the next leg. Card: **Clinic's aprons → Clinic. Finished before the move; never collected.** |
| **2 · The last order** | Depot → Clinic: mended aprons | The full South Cut is the natural road (S0, HJW, HOP or DOG, HJE, S1); the inner streets stay legal. Clinic receipt, Astra's, used verbatim: “Aprons received. Shorter ties this time—no more catching on the cupboard.” Clinic then offers: “Three for mending. Blue thread marks the torn pockets.” Card: **Clinic repairs → Tess · South counter. First order to her new address.** Depot's table is now clear. |
| **3 · First order in** | Clinic → south counter: repair bag | Her regulars now come to her. The counter the player left half-unpacked is open. |

**Tess at her counter, first meeting** (half-unpacked, jackets on the counter)

1. “You're the courier? I'm Tess. First day at my own counter.”
2. “Depot lent me a table for a year. Their jackets are mended—call it rent.”
3. “Clinic's aprons are waiting there too. I'm not leaving this counter on day one.”

**Tess at her counter, final beat** (open)

1. “You've brought my first order in. Clinic, of course.”
2. “Your cargo cover's split along the seam. You take every corner like that?”
3. “Pull in. This'll take a minute.”

*The patch appears on the craft and stays.* This is the chapter's one turn toward the courier. Tess is the first person who notices the player's own things, and she notices them through the way the player drives. She is amused, not disapproving. Replace “cargo cover” with whatever cloth or strap the craft actually has.

**Consequences**

- **The frontage.** The MENDING half of the existing TEA & MENDING sign lights up. Finished work hangs, and incoming and outgoing shelves separate.
- **Depot.** Its table is empty.
- **Routine.** Ordinary mending pickups now start at B11, and Tess becomes somewhere Relay can route requests.
- **The courier.** The craft carries her patch.

**Driving identity: lines.** Arc 1 was about committing to long roads. Arc 2 is inner-street precision, the South Cut as centerpiece, and a small new roadside approach. It is the first arc where Spread/Hop choices matter more than top speed.

**Left out on purpose.** No Works visit, no sign delivery, no hauling her furniture (she moved herself), no utility unlock, no second named person.

**Implementation notes**

- **Contact point.** B11 needs a roadside approach checked in the current build, outside the HOP/DOG lines.
- **The patch.** The cheapest version is a decal on an existing panel; a stitched strap or tag also works. If neither is cheap, the arc still functions, but this is the single new asset I'd fight for.
- **The jackets leg.** This is the suspected soft spot. If it plays as an errand, cut it and make the aprons a “collect at Depot” pickup.

## 5. Coda — “Rope isn't a repair”

This becomes available after Arc 2 plus any one other job, so time has visibly passed. Relay posts:

> **QUARRY STORES** — Tarp's split again. Strong thread and patches, please. It's held together with rope.
> **RELAY** — Ivo says there's a new mender down south. Try Tess. —R.

Card: **Patch kit → Quarry Stores. Pick up at Tess's counter.**

Tess, one panel: “Strong thread. And tell them rope isn't a repair.”

The leg runs west through the belt, out West Gate and along A0.

Quarry receipt: “Patches received. Rope's back on its hook. Come out when you're not working—we'll put the kettle on. —B.”

Without anyone explaining anything, this one leg:

- **Grows Arc 1's consequence.** Relay now routes a request across the city instead of answering from its own rack.
- **Confirms the tea visit.** One clause, “Ivo says,” does it. No tea scene gets written.
- **Stages Tess and Ivo's opposite reactions** to makeshift fixes, without them ever meeting.
- **Turns the courier's chapter around.** The chapter began with the courier carrying someone else's invitation; it ends with the courier receiving one.
- **Seeds the next portrait:** “—B.”

## 6. Optional return lines

These become available at Works and Relay after Arc 1 plus one other job. Each is one current-state sentence, never required:

- **Ivo:** “She came by after close. Sat an hour and reorganized my shelves.”
- **Ren:** “Went over to Ivo's. He talked about hinges for an hour.”

What these do:

- They confirm the visit happened.
- Each teases the other, which keeps the relationship a friendship with two sides rather than pining on one.
- Together they describe the same hour from two chairs.
- They measure attachment directly: did the player go back when nothing asked them to?

## 7. Arc 2 candidates, reviewed

**Tess: chosen, rewritten.** She tests what Arc 1 can't: the courier's own acquaintance, new geography, and a change with no machine in it.

Changes from the Shadow Study:

- **She is met at her new counter, not at Depot.** A Depot meeting followed by a move would recreate exactly the person–place split Charlie hit with Ren.
- **The human reason is her first day in her own place**, not a changed return address.
- **Her trait is reading people through wear, not guarding a workspace boundary.** A boundary keeps the player at arm's length on first meeting. “Customers that side, work this side” can come back later as a line of pride.
- **Her draft first line lost *today*,** which is Ren's word.
- **The arc now ends by turning toward the courier.**

Kept from the study: the South Cut leg, Clinic as her regular, and all of Astra's Clinic receipts.

**Sena: parked.** Mediating an equipment loan between institutions is the least human of the three situations. Its utility payoff would confound the attachment test, and her acceptance of wear overlaps Tess. Two things are worth keeping for later:

- The line “Still shuts. Leave their patch on it,” for when Clinic eventually gets a face.
- The idea of attaching the existing cargo-liner action to a person. Tess is the natural home for it later, since liners are cloth.

**Bea: right person, wrong first arc.** A display is static, it states its own message, and (as Astra notes) it barely uses Quarry's roads. Instead:

- **Let her emerge from the Quarry tickets.** She is the dry voice behind “drying on the kettle.”
- **Give her the portrait and arc in Chapter 2,** on the outside roads, with a load where care shows.
- **Keep her rough-edge pride, but aim it at use rather than display:** the crew's work placed somewhere the player passes every day, carrying the crew's mark.

**Anything better?** I considered a Depot freight hand, a Clinic courier who stopped coming, and a Market cooperative lead. None beats a rewritten Tess on the three things Arc 2 has to test.

**Anonymous voices, each kept consistent:**

- **Market dispatch.** The home voice: brisk and neutral. The place you start from and return to doesn't need a face yet.
- **Depot freight desk.** Gruff, and fond of Tess.
- **Clinic receiving.** Precise about fit and wear.
- **Quarry Stores.** Deadpan, speaking as “we.” The only institution allowed to be funny, until it becomes Bea.

**Environmental only:** the TEA half of B11 (a warm window, no dialogue), the Works chair, Relay's rack, Depot's table, the gates and junctions, the Water Office, the inland utility office, the outer roads.

Every important place gets a human identity over time. Until a story needs a person there, a stable receipt voice is identity enough.

## 9. Larger shapes (sketches, not lore)

**A · The round.** The bible says Ren once handled a freight round; this sketch proposes she drove it. Nobody took it over when she moved to the desk, so small things fall into gaps: Relay's mail stuck at Market, Clinic's aprons uncollected at Depot. Job by job, the courier ends up driving it.

- **The reveal.** Nobody says so until Ren does, late: “You know that's my old round?”
- **A later payoff.** The round's working tag, fixed to your craft.
- **What it does for play.** It gives route knowledge a name, and gives an optional challenge a person behind it: Ren's old time as a friendly mark.
- **Risk.** A successor myth, and too much weight on Ren. Keep it a job nobody wanted, not a destiny.

**B · Requests travel farther.** The services all work; they just don't reach each other. Each arc extends how far a request can go:

1. Relay answers from its own rack.
2. Relay routes a request to someone in the south.
3. Later, a shared order needs three places.

The evidence sits on the tickets themselves: who asked, who routed it, who filled it. *Risk:* this slides back into infrastructure repair, parts → install again. Make every new link a person agreeing to answer, not a device.

**C · Known by name.** This is about the courier's standing rather than the city's. Tickets move from “Courier” to “you.” Initials become people, and characters mention you to each other. Eventually a request arrives from someone you've never met, asking for you.

- **Aesthetic progression.** It pairs naturally with people leaving practical marks on the craft: Tess's patch now, perhaps Ren's tag later. Not every arc, and never a stat.
- **Risk.** Flattery. Keep it dry.

**Lean: A as the spine, C as its texture, B as the visible evidence.** The three are compatible, and none needs lore beyond one fact the bible already has. If C's name *is* A's round, the courier becomes known without the game inventing a personal history. Decide after the chapter test whether players picked up any of the seeds.

## 10. Story and driving difficulty

**Geography is the difficulty curve; story chooses the geography.** Later jobs get harder because of where people live and work, not because of new rules.

- **Every story leg has two routes:** a forgiving legal route and an expressive line. It can always be completed slowly; it's just better fast.
- **Longer Drive commitments** come as requests travel farther (shape B), until single legs cross the whole Basin. Quarry to Clinic, for example, runs A0, West Gate, S0, the Cut and S1.
- **Technical Spread/Hop geometry** comes from where later people work: up a narrow lane, or beside the Works conduit.
- **South Cut** is Arc 2's centerpiece, and later the natural road for anything Clinic-bound from the west.
- **Outer sweeps** are Relay's territory. The northern desk sends you wide.
- **Quarry Shelf (X0)** belongs to Bea's chapter. It becomes the natural road for Quarry ↔ Relay traffic once the two start talking.
- **Cargo care** applies only when the cargo really is delicate: a finished stone edge, not socks. Condition changes the receipt's wording and the optional bonus, never the outcome. Characters react; they don't grade. Bea, clean: “Not a mark on it.” Scuffed: “It's stone. It'll live.”
- **Route knowledge becomes social knowledge.** People name roads the way locals do (the Cut, the shelf, the Quarry run), and cards for familiar destinations can say less over time.
- **Optional mastery sits beside story as a character's offer.** Under shape A, that could be Ren's old west-sweep line. Taking it earns a reaction, never progress.
- **Never:** timers on story legs, mastery conditions on consequences, or emergencies invented to justify speed.

## 11. Tone check

**Sustainable, if “practical” carries the weight, not “kindness.”** The earlier phrase, *small practical kindnesses*, pulls writers toward gifts, gratitude and cozy nouns, which is where twee comes from. A better statement: **reckless, joyful driving for ordinary, practical reasons.**

Guardrails:

- **It's paid work.** People are customers first; kindness is incidental and occasional.
- **Everyone has an edge.** Ren overpromises, Ivo teases, Tess critiques your seams, Quarry is sardonic.
- **At most one thank-you per arc.**
- **Humor comes from practicality, not cuteness.** Rope as a repair; socks on a kettle. Wry, not cozy.
- **Hot drinks are rationed.** Tea is Ivo's, once. The kettle is Quarry's. Nobody else gets hot drinks this chapter.
- **Ordinary needs stay ordinary.** No emergency, and no moral attached.
- **Most jobs are just jobs:** one receipt line, no story. The story reads because it's rare.
- **The story notices the driving drily.** “You take every corner like that?” is amused, never disapproving, never cheering.
