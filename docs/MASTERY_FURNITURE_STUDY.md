# Furniture lane — retain the roads

This lane produced a placement study, not additional production geometry.
Fresh ordinary-camera views cover West Sweep entry/bend/exit, South Hop, Quarry
Shelf entry/turn and the Quarry approach. The accepted Works service bar and
South Hop/dogleg already produce a decision. West Sweep has a sustained bend;
Quarry Shelf has narrow road edges and changes of direction. Those existing
constraints are enough to test the revised route objectives before adding more.

Two possible thin wall plates were examined against the current wall polygons:

| Study | Candidate wall position (x,z m) | Decision it could clarify | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| West Sweep entry name | (-129.105, -4.403), inner retaining face | Where the timed road begins | Defer: eligible flat mounting face is about 42 m from the route sample and unlikely to give strong advance guidance at Drive speed |
| Quarry Shelf approach name | (-60.303, -223.788), outer retaining face | Begin the slower technical section | Defer: eligible flat face is about 33 m from the route sample; camera/readability benefit is unproved |

Positions were projections onto existing straight wall segments of at least
6 m, not all possible wall faces. The 4.5 × 1.6 m plate footprint was only a
data study; no rendered or colliding object was added. Exact inputs and fresh
views are retained in the review evidence. Existing route highlights plus the
active section readout will be evaluated first.

Additional Depot pylons were rejected: they would reduce a valued release
space without a demonstrated problem in the accepted drift challenge. Another
Works bar or South slalom was rejected: those locations already ask for mode,
braking or Hop decisions. The same applies to a new Shelf pinch until the
unmodified Shelf challenge has received owner play.

No terrain, road, collision, city mesh, weather or destination position changed.
If the section starts prove hard to recognize in play, one small approach cue
is a better next test than another physical obstacle. This remains an owner
readability question, not a claim that the present signage is sufficient.
